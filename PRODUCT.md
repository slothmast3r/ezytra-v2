# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Prospective clients evaluating a senior independent designer/developer for a serious web project — typically a founder, marketing lead, or studio owner who has already seen a dozen agency sites and is skeptical of polish that isn't backed by work. They land on ezytra.com after a referral, a search, or a link in correspondence. Their job to be done: decide, in under two minutes, whether Oskar is the real thing and worth a direct conversation. A secondary audience is Polish-market B2B buyers (dance and martial-arts school owners) arriving on `/pl/` landings with a sharper "can this person build me a site that converts" intent.

## Product Purpose

ezytra is the personal site and digital craft studio portfolio of Oskar Straszyński. It exists to make the case for working directly with a single craftsman — no handoffs, no markup — and to do so by demonstration rather than assertion. Success looks like a qualified prospect reading one case study end-to-end, then sending an email that already has a project scope attached. The site itself is the proof of work.

## Brand Personality

Precise, considered, direct. The voice is operator-grade: short sentences, concrete claims, no agency-speak. Emotional goal is **technical precision** — the impression that every line of type, every spacing decision, every motion curve is deliberate. Confidence without volume. Warm where appropriate (it is a person, not a firm), never folksy.

## Anti-references

- **Web3 / neon brutalism**: neon-on-black palettes, oversized type as pure decoration, deliberately chaotic grids, glitch effects. Wrong audience, wrong message.
- **Generic SaaS landing aesthetic**: hero metric template, three-column feature grid, gradient accents, cream-on-cream sections.
- **Agency template look**: stock photography, "we craft digital experiences" copy, parallax hero video, scrolljacking.
- **AI / glassmorphism reflex**: blurry glass cards, gradient meshes, generic Inter + purple, gradient text.

## Design Principles

1. **The site is the proof.** Every surface should be evidence that the person behind it can do the work being sold. Polish is the argument; testimonials and metrics are secondary.
2. **Earn the type.** Big typography is permitted only when it carries weight a smaller treatment couldn't. No decorative scale.
3. **Direct address.** Copy speaks from "I" to "you," never from "we" to "users." Specifics over abstractions, examples over claims.
4. **Restraint over reach.** One accent (warm gold) does the load-bearing work. Color, motion, and effects are spent sparingly, where they sharpen meaning.
5. **Show, don't tell.** Case studies lead with the artifact and the outcome. Process explanations are short and load behind the work, not in front of it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA across all surfaces. Verify warm-gold accent (`#e2d1a7`) contrast against `#0b0b0c` background meets AA for both large and body text; do not use the accent on muted gray. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — disable scroll-driven and parallax effects, keep only essential opacity/translate transitions. Keyboard navigation must reach every interactive element in visual reading order. Focus states must be visible on the dark surface (the same accent works as a focus ring at full chroma). Polish locale content must be lang-tagged correctly for assistive tech.
